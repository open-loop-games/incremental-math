# Bifurcations: where the algebra ends

The first six pages of this catalog walked shapes that close: power-law,
exponential, logistic, log, and the four composition rules that keep them closed
under sums, products, splices, and functional composition. This page walks the
complement — the rate-function shapes that don't close, and what the spec layer
reaches for instead. The catalog stops where the algebra stops; past that, the
math doesn't disappear, it changes language.

The first-passage frame survives every bifurcation. Each row below is still
"time to reach a milestone" in some generalized sense — mean hitting time,
equilibrium, threshold policy, optimum over a discrete option space. What
changes is the algebra of "rate" and the meaning of "cumulative." Each row
also has a body of named mathematics that gives a different shape of spec —
not "give up and simulate."

## The seven buckets

| Bucket                       | Rate-function shape that fails to close                                    | Named adjacent math                          |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Coupled-state deterministic  | Multi-resource mutual feedback, not linear and not triangular              | Phase plane, Lyapunov, bifurcation theory    |
| Strategy-space integration   | Adversarial multi-agent: equilibrium between strategies                    | Nash, replicator dynamics, best-response     |
| Stochastic load-bearing      | Distribution shape carries design intent, not just the mean                | Markov chains, phase-type, first-passage     |
| Discrete-event coupled       | Combinatorial trigger ordering across multiple subsystems                  | Combinatorial game theory, MDPs              |
| Network-topological          | Throughput bounded by graph topology, not rates                            | Max-flow min-cut, LP duality, network flows  |
| Optimal stopping             | When to reset is itself the decision                                       | Bellman equation, Snell envelope, free-boundary |
| Combinatorial selection      | Configuration choice from a discrete option space                          | Combinatorial optimization, search           |

## Coupled-state deterministic

When two or more resources mutually feed each other's rates and the coupling is
neither linear nor triangular — predator–prey, multi-species
[Lotka-Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotka%E2%80%93Volterra_equations),
multi-faction warfare — the rate-function algebra runs out. A closed-form
trajectory often still exists implicitly through a
[conserved quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_law), but the
time-parametrization $t(R)$ — when the orbit reaches a given point — does not.

The spec layer in this regime drops down to qualitative invariants:
[matrix exponentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_exponential) for the
linear case, [phase portraits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_portrait) and
[Lyapunov functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyapunov_function) for stable
equilibria, [bifurcation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bifurcation_theory)
for parameter-driven shape changes. Most idle/incremental games avoid genuinely
coupled multi-resource systems for exactly this reason — the games that ship
them either restrict to linear dynamics or simplify the coupling to a
triangular dependency that closes per the
[composition rules](/rate-functions/composition#what-doesnt-compose-cleanly).

## Strategy-space integration

When a "rate" depends on what an opponent does — tug-of-war faction warfare,
PvP-idle drafting from a shared pool — the per-fixed-strategy ROI closes per
the algebra, but the equilibrium between strategies is a fixed point of the
[best-response operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_response):
$\sigma^* = BR(\sigma^*)$. Fixed points of operators are not in general
closed-form, even when the operator is. Outside small special cases (zero-sum,
potential, supermodular games), Nash computation is
[PPAD-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PPAD_(complexity)).

The spec layer shifts to the *game form* — strategy spaces, payoff functions,
information structure — and a claim about which equilibrium concept applies:
[Nash equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_equilibrium) when unique,
[replicator dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replicator_equation) from a
uniform start when not. The bucket is rare in the genre because most idle games
are cooperative or single-player; it activates with adversarial multi-agent
strategy spaces over shared resource draws.

## Stochastic load-bearing

When a production mechanic's *distribution shape* — not just its mean — carries
the design intent. Mean first-passage and stationary distribution close for
finite [Markov chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_chain) (the linear
system $Q \cdot m = -1$); the full distribution of first-passage times rarely
does, even for a random walk hitting a barrier (the hitting-time distribution
involves [Bessel functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel_function)).

The spec is the distribution family — three named ones cover most of the
genre's territory:
[geometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_distribution) for trials
until first success (gacha pity timers' natural model);
[exponential / gamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_distribution) for
continuous-time waits in Poisson-like systems;
[phase-type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase-type_distribution) for
finite-state continuous-time Markov-chain hitting times, flexible enough to
cover most game-mechanic randomness with closed-form mean, variance, and
Laplace transform. The dimensionless feel-knob is the
[coefficient of variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficient_of_variation)
$\sigma/\mu$.

with random per-tick updates — the distribution shape (periodic bounded drift,
not stationary) is the design knob, not a calibration of the mean:

## Discrete-event coupled

When the state space is combinatorial in the count of units, cards, or
resources, and multiple subsystems interact through event ordering — auto-battler
combat with on-attack / on-hurt / on-faint / on-kill triggers chained across
both sides, deck-builders with stacking status effects. The rule-set closes as
a state graph; a closed form for the value function across the whole space is
rare, and the trajectory of an actual play is rarer still because of branch
contingencies.

The spec layer reaches for the game structure rather than a closed-form value:
[combinatorial game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_game_theory)
with [Sprague-Grundy values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprague%E2%80%93Grundy_theorem)
for impartial games, [Markov decision processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_decision_process)
with value iteration for small state spaces, value bounds where exact values
elude. A common spec-form for combat specifically is "expected damage per round
given the army composition," computed by discrete-event simulation but
expressible as a vector-matrix product over a finite event lattice.

The bucket is structurally rare in the genre — most "discrete-event" runtimes
in idle/incremental games are integrating closed specs (per-tower DPS,
per-weapon DPS, per-faction creep rate) rather than combinatorially branching
in their *outcome*.

## Network-topological

When throughput is bounded by graph topology, not by per-rate formulas — supply
chains, routing through a maze, RAM allocation across a server network. The
linear case closes:
[max-flow equals min-cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-flow_min-cut_theorem)
by [linear programming duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duality_(optimization)),
and [Dijkstra's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijkstra%27s_algorithm)
for shortest paths runs in polynomial time. The two failure modes are nonlinear
costs (capacity or cost depending on flow — congestion games, Wardrop equilibria
push the problem into NLP) and combinatorial constraints (integer-valued flows
in NP-hard problems admit only approximations and bounds).

The spec layer is the graph and capacities (or capacity functions) plus the
optimality criterion. The first-passage analog reads as "time to deliver $C$
units from source to sink given throughput bound $\text{cap}_{\min}$" — for a
saturated network, throughput is the min-cut value, and design knobs are the
graph topology and per-edge capacities. Industry Idle is the corpus's clearest
exemplar — buildings as nodes, input/output sets as edges, an explicit graph
walk for reachability:

## Optimal stopping

When *when to reset* is itself the decision; the value function is the supremum
over [stopping times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stopping_time):

$$V(x) = \sup_\tau \mathbb{E}\!\left[ e^{-\rho \tau} \cdot g(X_\tau) \mid X_0 = x \right]$$

Threshold policies close in special cases —
[geometric Brownian motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_Brownian_motion)
with discounted exponential payoffs, finite-horizon discounted MDPs with
special structure,
[Gittins indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gittins_index) for multi-armed
bandits with discounting. The general case is a
[free-boundary problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_boundary_problem) on
the [Bellman equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellman_equation), with
the optimal stopping time given by the
[Snell envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snell_envelope) — a smooth-pasting
condition at the threshold separating "continue" from "stop."

The threshold-policy spec reads as: "prestige when post-reset growth rate ×
multiplier exceeds pre-reset rate by factor $X$," with $X$ computable from the
rate function and discount. Antimatter Dimensions ships a concrete instance —
the Big Crunch autobuyer's `xHighest` ratio mode triggers when the current
run's IP exceeds $X$ times the previous run's best:

geometric-compression growth curve is unproven. That gap is what the bucket
covers.

## Combinatorial selection

When the player chooses a subset, sequence, or arrangement from a discrete
option space — Time Studies on a 91-node DAG, action queues over a prereq-gated
action set, spatial inventory arrangements, faction-and-perk loadouts. Per
fixed selection the system closes (modifier chains collapse to constant
multipliers; action durations sum); across selections the optimization is a
[combinatorial optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_optimization)
or constraint-satisfaction problem on the option space.

This bucket sits adjacent to Optimal stopping — both are about strategic
decisions — but the boundary is whether the decision is a *stopping time* or a
*configuration*. Prestige timing is stopping; choosing which Time Studies to
buy or which actions to queue is configuration. The named adjacent math is
search and pruning ([branch-and-bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branch_and_bound),
[A\*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A*_search_algorithm), constraint
propagation) rather than the variational machinery of stopping.

Idle Loops is the corpus's clearest exemplar of configuration-as-rate-function.
The player authors a plan over a prereq-gated action set; a cursor walks the
plan repeatedly; per fixed plan total mana spend is a sum, but the *plan
itself* is the design space:

## What stays mathematical

Each bucket above gives a *named alternative math* with its own catalog of
theorems and methods. The spec layer doesn't dissolve at the bifurcation; it
changes language. A spec that reads "best-response equilibrium with replicator
dynamics," or "phase-type distribution with mean $\mu$ and CV $\sigma/\mu$," or
"min-cut throughput on a graph with capacities $c_{ij}$" is still precise,
still durable, still the right thing to write down before tuning.

The failure mode this page exists to prevent isn't "the math runs out" — it's
"sim takes over." Throwing a [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_method)
at a stochastic subsystem is calibration, not spec; the spec is the
distribution family. Throwing brute-force tree search at an adversarial
subsystem is calibration; the spec is the equilibrium concept. The distinction
matters for the same reason it matters in the closed-form regime: the spec
compresses the design space and tells you which knobs are independent.

The catalog stops where the algebra stops, and the seven rows above are the
boundary the design space turns at.
