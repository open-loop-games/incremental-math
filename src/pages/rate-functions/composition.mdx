# Composition rules

The first four pages of this catalog walked single-shape rate functions: power-law,
exponential, logistic, log. Real systems compose. The question this page answers: which
compositions of catalog entries stay closed-form, and which fall off the algebra?

The answer breaks into four kinds of composition. The first two — piecewise splice and
modifier-product — get their own demos
because the genre's headline patterns sit on top of them. The other two —
sum and
[functional composition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_composition) — get
formulas and notes; the spec layer's behavior under each is well-defined enough that a
demo would only restate the math.

## Piecewise splice

Different rate function on different intervals, glued continuously at breakpoints.

$$r(x) = r_i(x) \;\text{ on } [x_i, x_{i+1}), \quad R(x) = R(x_i) + \int_{x_i}^x r_i(s)\,ds$$

Closure is automatic per regime — each $r_i$ is one of the catalog's entries and integrates
on its own. Inversion needs regime detection: $T(C)$ is itself a piecewise function of $C$,
with breakpoints at $C_i = R(x_i)$. As long as the regime-boundary cumulatives can be
precomputed, the global first-passage is a regime lookup followed by an
intra-regime inversion.

The genre's lead piecewise pattern is "two geometrics glued at a threshold": a normal-paced
ratio before some level, a slower ratio past it. Realm Grinder's pre/post-Ascension cost
shift is the canonical example — $c_0 \cdot 1.15^n$ for $n \le t$, $c_0 \cdot 1.15^t \cdot
1.03^{n-t}$ for $n > t$. Antimatter Dimensions ships the same pattern as a parameterized
class — pre-threshold is the geometric ladder, post-threshold the per-step ratio shifts:

<Cite repo="AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode" path="src/core/math.js" line="320-352" />

```js
window.ExponentialCostScaling = class ExponentialCostScaling {
  // baseCost B, baseIncrease C, costScale S, purchasesBeforeScaling n.
  // Pre-scaling: B, B·C, B·C², ..., B·C^n.
  // Post-scaling: B·C^(n+1), B·C^(n+2)·S, B·C^(n+3)·S^3, ...
};
```

The threshold is `purchasesBeforeScaling`; one parameter selects which regime applies.
The cumulative is the prefix sum of one of two geometric series — closed-form per
regime, with regime detection on $n$ — exactly the piecewise structure formalized in
code.

What the rate panel makes visible (on log y-axis): the kink at $x = t$ is a slope change in
[log-linear coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log%E2%80%93linear_plot) — both
regimes are straight lines, just at different angles. On linear y-axis the kink would be
swamped by the exponential blowup; on log it's the visual headline. The first-passage
panel shows the same kink in $T(C)$, at the cumulative threshold $R(t)$ rather than the
input threshold $t$ — the regime change happens at a _budget_ level, not at a _level_
level, which is what the player feels.

## Modifier-product (state-independent multipliers)

A multiplier applied to a rate, where the multiplier doesn't itself depend on the rate's
state.

$$r(x) = r_{\text{base}}(x) \cdot \prod_i \mu_i, \quad R(x) = \left(\prod_i \mu_i\right) \cdot R_{\text{base}}(x)$$

The cumulative is just the base cumulative scaled by the modifier product. The
first-passage rescales inversely:

$$T(C) = T_{\text{base}}\!\left(\frac{C}{\prod_i \mu_i}\right)$$

The key invariant: **modifiers on rates are linear in the cumulative**. The shape doesn't
change; the timescale does. This is why a many-multiplier system stays tractable — every
modifier is a coordinate change on the rate, not a transformation of its shape. The
[modifier chains page](/modern/modifier-chains) walks the design pattern; the
[playbook's composition page](/design-playbook/composition) walks the design *consequence*
of this rule (mechanics that compose multiplicatively don't go obsolete the way additive
mechanics do); the math underneath both is exactly this composition.

The constraint is "state-independent." A modifier that depends on the _same state_ the rate
depends on — say, a multiplier that grows as $x$ grows — is no longer a modifier-product;
it's a different rate function. That case is the
[coupled-state row](/rate-functions/bifurcations#coupled-state-deterministic) on
the bifurcations page.

the modifier, divide the time-to-threshold by three.

## Sum

$$r(x) = r_1(x) + r_2(x), \quad R(x) = R_1(x) + R_2(x)$$

Closure under sum is automatic. Each cumulative integrates on its own; the combined
cumulative is the sum. Inversion is what's interesting: $R_1(x) + R_2(x) = C$ may not
have an elementary solution even when each $R_i$ does, because solving a sum of named
functions is its own nontrivial algebra.

The cleanest case is when one term [dominates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_analysis)
in the regime of interest. The catalog's asymptotic ordering at infinity is

$$\text{constant} \ll \log \ll \text{polynomial} \ll \text{exponential}$$

and at the origin the order reverses. So a rate with a polynomial term plus a constant
term is essentially the polynomial in the late game and essentially the constant in the
early game — the cumulative inverts approximately closed-form by picking the dominant term
per regime and stitching at the crossover.

What this gives the spec layer: even when the _exact_ first-passage isn't elementary, the
_regime-wise_ first-passage is. Designers pick which shape dominates in each regime;
the sum just decides where the regimes are.

## Functional composition

$$r(x) = r_1(g(x))$$

Closure is preserved when $g$ is monotone and elementary, and $R_1(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$
integrates elementarily by the chain rule. The most useful instance: $g(x) = \ln x$
substitutes log-shape into polynomial-shape, and several otherwise-non-elementary integrals
collapse under that substitution. The reverse — substituting polynomial into log — usually
makes things worse; not every substitution closes.

In a design context, functional composition shows up most often as **prestige math**: the
prestige currency $H$ is a function of the primary cumulative $C$, the per-prestige rate
runs against a function of $H$, and the post-prestige run is the cumulative integrated
against the modified rate. The whole stack is closed-form if each layer is one of the
catalog's entries and each layer's $g$ is monotone elementary. When it isn't, the
prestige math falls into the
[optimal-stopping row](/rate-functions/bifurcations#optimal-stopping) of the
bifurcations page — the underlying cycle is an
[optimal-stopping problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_stopping) that
doesn't close, even when each individual run does.

## What doesn't compose cleanly

The catalog's main hand-wave: not every product of catalog entries closes. The
named adjacent math takes over in eight rows — coupled-state deterministic,
strategy-space integration, stochastic load-bearing, discrete-event coupled,
network-topological, bilevel interdiction, optimal stopping, combinatorial
selection — each with its own spec vocabulary. The full census, with corpus exemplars and the named
mathematics each row hands off to, lives on the
[bifurcations page](/rate-functions/bifurcations).

## Composition recap

| Composition                    | Closes when                                    | Spec form                             |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Piecewise splice               | Each regime closes                             | $T(C)$ piecewise on $C$               |
| Modifier-product (state-indep) | Always                                         | $T_{\text{base}}(C / \prod \mu_i)$    |
| Sum                            | Cumulative always; inversion regime-wise       | $R_1 + R_2$, dominant-term per regime |
| Functional composition         | $g$ monotone elementary, chain rule integrates | $T = g^{-1}(T_{\text{base}})$         |
| Triangular multi-resource      | Each scalar closes in dependency order         | Solve in sequence                     |

These five lines are the catalog's composition algebra. Where a system's shape
sits outside them, the spec layer hands off to the
[bifurcations page](/rate-functions/bifurcations) and a different vocabulary —
asymptotic envelopes,
[best-response dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_response),
[matrix exponentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_exponential),
phase-type distributions, min-cut throughput, threshold policies. Closed-form
spec is the destination wherever it can be reached; where it can't, the math
just changes shape.
